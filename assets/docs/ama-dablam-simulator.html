Ama Dablam Summit Day Simulator

Ama Dablam, Southwest Ridge. Every dot is a person on the summit push from Camp 2 (about 6,000 m, on top of the Yellow Tower) to the top (6,812 m) and back. The whole route is fixed rope, and the Mushroom Ridge traverse above Camp 2 is one person at a time in both directions. Built from the Himalayan Database the same way as the Everest simulator from The Summit Clock. Companion to the Ama Dablam report.

The slider sets people on the rope per client. 1.69× is the historical ratio: clients were 59% of everyone on the rope on Ama Dablam’s busy days (20+ people, Himalayan Database 1990–2025).

47 on the rope is the busiest day in the Himalayan Database (9 Nov 2024, and again 27 Oct 2025). On the weekend of 24–25 Oct 2026 more than 200 people were reported heading for the summit, roughly 100 a day. 178 is the autumn 2026 permit count on 13 October: a stress test, not a forecast.

Auto is 3.0 minutes a person (20 an hour), just above the busiest hour ever recorded on top (19 summits in an hour). A team of one client and its staff leaves together.

Operators now fix double ropes on much of the route so people can go up and down at the same time (ExplorersWeb, Oct 2026). Each direction is still single file.

Ama Dablam has never been crowded enough to measure its rope speed: the most summits in one hour was 19, and busy days were not slower. These three speeds are assumptions, all above that observed peak.

Their plan from the 26 Oct live stream: Base Camp to Camp 1 on 27 Oct, summit gear cached at Camp 1 or 2, a light, fast push for a sunrise summit on 28 Oct, then rappel and a fast descent. Departure time and pace are estimates until they say.

Fastest tenth from Camp 2 in the record: 5.4 h; fastest quarter 6.5 h; median 8 h. Descent set 30% faster than average (rappelling, no clients).

The model counts only deaths on the way down linked to the summit hour. The record also holds what no model predicts: in November 2006 a serac fell from the Dablam onto Camp 3 and killed six people in their tents, and falling rock and ice hit climbers on the ridge (rockfall killed a climber in 2025). Those are in the “any cause” line, and more people on the rope means more people under anything that falls.

Model: people climb from Camp 2 to the start of the Mushroom Ridge in about 2.5 hours at their own pace, then pass one at a time (2.5 min each going up, 2.0 going down, plus 1.5 min each time traffic changes direction; slow 3.5 / 2.8 / 2, fast 1.5 / 1.2 / 1), then about 5 hours to the top. 15 minutes on top, about 4.3 hours back to Camp 2 (the recorded median is 4.5). Risk of dying on the way down comes from the summit hour (each hour later multiplies the odds by 1.37, the 8,000 m slope) with the level set to Ama Dablam’s own record: 2.7 per 1,000 for a client on top at noon. Hired staff carry about a quarter of a client’s odds; anyone turned back carries 2.4 per 1,000. Weather does not change anyone’s pace or risk in the model; it is shown alongside the clock. A comparison tool, not a forecast of a real day.
