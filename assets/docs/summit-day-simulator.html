Summit Day Simulator

Everest, South Col route. Every dot is a person on the summit push from Camp 4 (7,900 m) to the top (8,849 m) and back. The summit section from the South Summit to the top is one fixed rope used in both directions. The model is the one in The Summit Clock, tuned to the 2016 and 2019 record days. Written up in Mountains, Part 6.

The slider sets people on the rope per client. 2.27× is the historical ratio: clients were 44% of everyone on the South Col rope on days with 150+ people (Himalayan Database, 1990–2025).

Auto is 2.0 minutes a person (about 30 an hour), the rate the summit took on the busiest recorded days. A team of one client and its staff leaves together.

The model counts only deaths on the way down linked to the summit hour. The record also holds what no model predicts: the 2014 Icefall avalanche killed 15 people on this route and the 2015 earthquake avalanche at Base Camp killed 14, and a bad season can bring a cluster of altitude illness. Those are in the “any cause” line, and they can happen on any day.

Model: people climb from Camp 4 to the South Summit in about 9 hours at their own pace, then pass one at a time through the summit section (0.8 min each going up, 0.64 going down, plus 1 min each time traffic changes direction; congested fit 1.0 / 0.8 / 1 min and 8.5 h). 20 minutes on top, about 5 hours back to Camp 4. Risk of dying on the way down comes from the summit hour (each hour later multiplies the odds by 1.37) for a 40-year-old client on oxygen; hired staff carry about a quarter of a client’s odds; anyone turned back carries 4.6 per 1,000. No weather, rescues or oxygen failures. A comparison tool, not a forecast of a real day.
